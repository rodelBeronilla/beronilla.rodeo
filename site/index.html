Principles

Four, wherever I am.

The data comes before the answer.

I go at a problem like a researcher. I’ll push an approach further than most people would, and when the evidence finally refuses, I stop arguing with it, ask the people around me and take another route.

  • Say only what is true

    Integrity and transparency, in practice. I say what I actually know, give credit where it belongs, and own my part when something goes wrong. If a claim needs stretching to sound good, it isn’t mine to make.

    Trust is slow to earn and quick to lose, and I’d rather be believed than impressive.

  • Get the question right

    Context before action. I want to understand what’s really being asked, and why, before I commit to anything, and I’ll keep asking until it’s clear. Once it is, I’m all in.

    Most wasted effort starts as a good answer to the wrong question.

  • Start from the data

    Curiosity with discipline. I’d rather sit with the evidence until the shape of a problem is obvious than defend a solution I reached for too early. When the data disagrees with me, the data wins.

    Being right early feels good. Being right in the end is what actually helps anyone.

  • Take something from everything

    Every job, team and mistake leaves something worth keeping. I pay attention to what each one taught me and carry it into the next thing.

    The parts that were uncomfortable at the time are the ones I still use.
